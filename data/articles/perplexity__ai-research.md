AI research tools can be split into two broad categories: general-use chatbots with research features and dedicated platforms for assistance with academic research.

Most professionals doing non-academic research will find that a general-purpose AI tool suits them best. For academics, there are strong similarities between the major providers. Which tool you choose will likely come down to task scope and preferences around task small features.

Plan out your research process using conventional methods. Then ask where AI might be able to handle discovery, synthesis, and analysis tasks.

Calculate the time it will take to write prompts and verify findings. AI can easily save hours or days on large literature reviews. But for smaller, focused questions, there is little advantage to a manual search.

Determine whether a specialized research platform offers any benefits over a general-purpose assistant like Perplexity. Pay attention to individual research features and coverage in your domain.

If you go for a dedicated tool, pick one that’s designed for your specific tasks. Tools like Elicit support structured literature reviews, for example, while Scite analyzes how academic papers are cited.

There are two things to pay attention to when looking at a provider’s terms and conditions. First, the use of

data and whether or not it is kept entirely confidential. Second, conditions covering ownership of outputs.

Don’t assume that a paid tier automatically confers data confidentiality or unrestricted usage rights.

Will your inputs include sensitive data, copyrighted documents, confidential business information, or unpublished findings?

It is important to check that you can feed these to an LLM. Various restrictions may be in place, from data regulations in your jurisdiction to internal company policy. You may need to find out how a particular tool provider stores data and for how long. Use of an approved enterprise platform may be necessary.

There’s an art to putting together good research prompts. Clearly articulate your research question, provide relevant context, and define the output format. For complex research tasks, you may need to break the question down into sub-prompts.

Ask for clear references to all sources used, even if they are not labelled in the output. This is important, as an AI-generated response may not include citations for all sources used.

It is essential to verify claims against original sources. These may be research papers, original research, official statistics, or technical documentation. Pay special attention to numerical data, dates, quotations, and time-sensitive information that may be out of date.

When doing research, you shouldn’t assume that because a source is cited that the resulting claim is correct or even that the source exists. For complex research, this stage is where you verify that the nuance of claims and summaries is correct.

Consult the disclosure requirements of your employer, academic institution, or relevant professional body. You will find details about the extent to which you need to disclose AI use.

Fields with strict regulations and codes of practice tend to require more detailed disclosure. Keep records of AI-assisted activities (platforms will usually keep an audit trail).

Research assistance has become one of the primary use cases for AI, and it’s easy to see why. It can handle large volumes of complex information, automate time-consuming repetitive tasks, and act as a thinking partner for early-stage ideas and hypotheses.

However, issues remain. In particular, the ethical use of AI in research is currently one of the most active and contested debates among academic institutions.

Hallucination rates correspond to topical complexity, and many leading engineers view them as a hard problem that can’t be fully solved. In addition, AI use is known to erode thinking skills, which are of paramount importance to professionals involved in complex research.

Organizations may also prohibit AI use on privacy and intellectual property grounds or over concerns around sensitive data. And disclosure remains important so that reviewers and peers can understand how AI was used in the research process.

AI will only continue to become a normal part of daily life in both the private and public spheres. As such, it is highly likely that clearer rules around research integrity and attribution will continue to evolve and gain nuance.
