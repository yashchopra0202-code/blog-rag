On July 24, 2025, Microsoft Research Asia – Singapore (MSRA – Singapore) opened its doors as Microsoft’s first research lab in Southeast Asia. The launch built on more than two decades of collaboration between Microsoft Research Asia and Singapore’s universities, research institutes, and government agencies. It marked a shift to a closer, more integrated relationship aimed at supporting Singapore’s innovation ecosystem and advancing the development and beneficial use of AI.

Established in 1998, MSRA has a presence in Beijing, Shanghai, Vancouver, and Tokyo, along with Singapore. The Singapore lab strengthens the collaboration with local partners and extends the scientific and societal impact of Microsoft’s global research community.

Over the past year, the Singapore lab has grown its team, deepened partnerships, and explored how frontier AI research can create real-world value. Guided by its mission to advance frontier AI for humanity, its work spans four pillars: next-generation AI models and agentic systems, domain-specific AI for real-world impact, AI-native research practices, and ecosystem and talent development.

Research has its greatest impact when it moves beyond the laboratory into practice: in health and medicine, enterprises, and the daily operations of organizations solving real problems. MSRA – Singapore aims to do just that through a two-way exchange: partners bring domain expertise, real-world data, and operational challenges, while the lab contributes frontier research and engineering capabilities. Together, they translate fundamental advances into practical solutions, while surfacing new questions that feed back into research in a kind of research-to-impact flywheel. Advances in AI foundations enable more capable models and agentic systems that can reason, plan, use tools, and collaborate with people. These capabilities create new opportunities for AI-native innovation. In turn, real-world applications reveal limitations and generate new questions, feeding insights back into foundational research and sustaining a continuous cycle of discovery, application, and learning.

Healthcare offers an early example of the lab’s research-to-impact model. Research on multimodal healthcare AI and self-evolving diagnostic agents is deployed and extended through partnerships across Singapore’s healthcare ecosystem. The lab is collaborating on multimodal and agentic AI approaches to support clinical decision-making, unlock insights from complex medical data, and advance research in disease diagnosis, risk assessment, and personalized care, with the longer-term goal of enabling meaningful real-world use.

Beyond healthcare, the lab is working with organizations across financial services, education, and technology to explore how frontier AI can address sector-specific needs and operational challenges.

In addition to its deep ties with government agencies, MSRA has built strong connections with Singapore’s academic community, supporting more than 75 research projects with Singapore institutions since 2004. The opening of the Singapore lab has brought new engagements, with nine new projects in its first year with National University of Singapore (NUS) and Nanyang Technological University, Singapore (NTU) across healthcare AI, robotics, AI systems, and multi-agent systems.

For Microsoft Research Asia, talent development is not a standalone program but a long-term investment in Singapore’s research and innovation capacity, supporting a comprehensive talent pipeline spanning internships, joint PhD supervision, visiting scholar programs, and fellowship initiatives. Through these opportunities, students and early-career researchers gain access to world-class mentorship, a curiosity-driven culture, frontier research challenges, and a global network connecting academia, industry, and real-world applications.

As Microsoft Research Asia — Singapore enters its second year, the focus is shifting from building foundations to scaling the strongest models of collaboration and demonstrating their impact more broadly.

One year ago, the lab opened with a mission to advance frontier AI in close partnership with the ecosystem around it. That mission is already taking shape through joint research, talent development, and the first partnerships connecting lab work to real-world challenges.

The year ahead is about scaling what works. By deepening these connections, Microsoft Research Asia — Singapore can bring more frontier research into practice and create meaningful value for Singapore, the wider region, industry, and society.
